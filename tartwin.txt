TAR Twin — Full Methodology
1. Research objective

The objective is to transform TAR from a static/periodic geopolitical risk indicator into a dynamic procurement decision system that continuously estimates:

What is happening now?
How strongly is the disruption propagating?
How long could it persist?
What will happen to a specific procurement exposure?
What would happen under alternative decisions?
What should the procurement manager do now?

The conceptual chain is:

Evidence→TAR→Market/OperationalResponse→LatentDisruptionState→Future−StateDistribution→ProcurementSimulation→OptimalAction
	​

2. The three components

I would formally define them as follows.

Component	Type	Primary question
TAR Candle	State visualization	What happened to the disruption state during this period?
Market Stress Index (MSI)	Market-response measurement	How strongly is the market pricing the disruption?
TAR Twin	Dynamic state + forecasting + simulation architecture	Given the current state, what happens next and what should procurement do?

So:

TAR Candle ≠ Recovery Module
MSI ≠ TAR
TAR Twin ≠ TAR Candle

They are layers of one system.

28. Phase 1 — Build TAR at high frequency

You need to determine whether the existing TAR methodology can legitimately operate at hourly/sub-daily frequency.

Do not assume that because articles arrive hourly, the predictive relationship is hourly.

12-hour
daily

You need an ablation study.

Market variables only.

TAR only.

Full TAR Twin.

Does each additional layer actually improve forecasting or procurement decisions?

This prevents the Twin becoming an impressive-looking collection of correlated indicators.

39. Avoid leakage

This will be absolutely critical.

Suppose you're predicting market stress at:

14:00

You cannot use information published at:

15:00.

Every observation must be timestamped according to when it became available, not simply its publication date or database date.

Likewise, for procurement decisions:

The model can only use information available at decision time.

This should be enforced through a point-in-time data architecture.
