TAR Tool — Recommended Upgrade Specification
0. First principle: preserve the existing architecture

The current tool already has a strong structure:

TAR signal → regime → timing validity → customer economics → Act / Wait

Do not replace this with a generic risk dashboard.

The current HTML already implements several important ideas:

customer-specific action cost
3/6/12-month windows
three policy alternatives
economic threshold
corridor selection
active-disruption override
track record
caveats and attribution
local decision logging

For example, the existing engine explicitly compares “Act every cycle,” “Follow the alert,” and “Never act.”

That logic should remain the foundation.

1. Reposition the product
Current positioning

Maritime war risk · act-or-wait decision tool

Keep this as the product category, but strengthen the proposition.

Recommended positioning

War-Risk Timing Intelligence

Main headline

Act now, or wait?

Subheadline

TAR converts geopolitical escalation into an economically actionable decision based on your exposure, mitigation cost, and the historical performance of the signal.

Important distinction

Do not market TAR as:

“AI predicts war.”

Do not claim:

“TAR predicts when a war will happen.”

Instead:

TAR evaluates whether a current risk signal contains enough historical decision value to justify early action.

This is much more defensible.

2. Separate the product into three analytical layers

Layer 2 — Timing validity

Answer:

Is this signal actually usable as an anticipatory warning?

Possible states:

VALID

Signal is generated before the disruption.

CONCURRENT

Signal is elevated but the disruption has already started.

INVALID

Current reading cannot support a timing decision.

INSUFFICIENT DATA

Not enough observations to evaluate timing.

This is important because your current engine already correctly prevents a disruption that is already underway from being treated as a future warning.

Keep that feature.

Make it much more visible.

4. Replace the single confidence concept with multiple confidence dimensions

Don't simply show:

Confidence: 82%

That would imply a precision the model may not possess.

Instead show:

Signal confidence

How strong is the TAR signal?

Timing confidence

How reliable is its historical lead time?

Attribution confidence

How directly can the signal be attributed to this corridor?

Data completeness

How complete is the underlying data?

Example:

MODEL QUALITY

Signal strength       HIGH
Timing evidence       MODERATE
Attribution            PROXY
Data completeness      HIGH

Your current dataset already distinguishes direct and proxy attribution and includes caveats.

Expose that information rather than hiding it.

5. Upgrade the customer cost input

This is one of the biggest commercial improvements.

Currently the customer effectively enters:

early action costs X% of crisis replacement.

That is mathematically elegant, but most customers won't naturally know this percentage.

Instead create:

"Calculate your early-action cost"

Let the customer enter actual costs.

Example
EARLY ACTION COST

Alternative supplier             €420,000
Additional freight               €180,000
Inventory buffer                  €75,000
Hedging                            €40,000
Insurance premium                  €25,000

-----------------------------------------
Total early-action cost          €740,000

Crisis replacement cost        €5,000,000

-----------------------------------------

Your action cost                  14.8%

Then:

α = €740,000 / €5,000,000 = 14.8%

The user never needs to understand α.

The engine does.

19. Implement walk-forward evaluation

The validation engine should operate like:

Train → predict → observe outcome

This is especially important for:

41. What I would NOT do

This is just as important.

❌ Don't add AI chat just because it looks impressive.
❌ Don't add 50 geopolitical indicators.
❌ Don't turn it into a generic geopolitical dashboard.
❌ Don't show fake precision like “73.4% probability of war.”
❌ Don't call every TAR spike a forecast.
❌ Don't hide false positives.
❌ Don't optimize the model after seeing the outcome.
❌ Don't let the UI imply that high TAR automatically means “Act.”
❌ Don't expand to 50 corridors before validating the first 5–7.
❌ Don't spend another month polishing CSS before fixing validation.
